\title{causalgeo: concurrent geo holdouts for bundle lift and calibrated channel responses}

\author[aff1]{Aditya Puttaparthi Tirumala\corref{cor1}}
\ead{puttaparthy.aditya@gmail.com}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\address[aff1]{Independent Researcher}

\begin{abstract}
Marketing teams often turn off different bundles of channels in different groups of markets at the same time to obtain enough statistical power. A geo holdout then gives the lift of each bundle, but it cannot say how much of that lift belongs to each channel. A marketing-mix model can estimate attribution at a channel level, though that split is unstable when channels move together. \texttt{causalgeo} is a Python package for this design. It estimates one lift per bundle, checks whether a channel meant to stay on delivered differently in the dark markets, and then fits a straight-line model of channel impressions that is pulled toward the measured bundle lifts, and it leaves a channel blank when the cells and impressions cannot separate it from the others. We test the package on Google's public Meridian impression paths with constructed sales, so the true responses are known. When the sales follow the straight-line model that the package fits, the recovered responses are within 0.21 percent of the planted values. On a separate 30-week series, the 95 percent cell intervals covered the true lift in 91.8 percent of 200 redrawn assignments. The checks also show where the split fails. A response that differs by market lowers coverage to 34.4 percent, a confounder left on keeps a response about 50 percent high, and leaving out carryover or saturation gives large channel errors. No real campaign and no comparison with other tools is included.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and significance}
\label{sec:motivation}

A geo test estimates incrementality by comparing markets that receive a media change with similar markets left on business as usual \cite{vaver2011,vaver2012}. Comparing markets avoids the bias that comes from lining up people who saw an ad with people who did not \cite{lewis2011,gordon2019}, and it asks whether the channel created incremental sales \cite{blake2015}.

In practice only some markets can go dark. Consider a United States campaign across 210 designated market areas (DMAs) where the business allows a pause in only 30. Large markets may be too risky to pause, and some regions have partner commitments or are already in a flight. Advertising lift is small next to ordinary sales movement \cite{lewisrao2015}, so a well-powered test of one channel would use the whole pool. Teams therefore test bundles of channels in several cells at once, and each cell gives the lift of its bundle and nothing about the channels inside it.

GeoLift and Meridian GeoX can design and estimate more than one cell \cite{geolift,geox2026}. To our knowledge neither splits a cell into channels. A mix model estimates channel response from impression history, and when channels move together that split is arbitrary \cite{chanperry2017,jin2017}. Robyn and PyMC-Marketing can pull a mix model toward a lift test \cite{runge2024,pymcmarketing2024}. Pulling toward a test can steady a split \cite{zhang2023}, but it cannot add information that the test and the impression data do not contain. The split in this paper also assumes that the bundle lift is the sum of the channel lifts. If channels interact, these equations cannot identify the separate responses \cite{naik2003}.

\texttt{causalgeo} estimates the bundle lift from the holdout and then uses that lift to guide a straight-line model of channel impressions. The channel responses therefore come from both the test and the model, and they are not channel-level holdout estimates. When the impressions cannot separate the channels, the package reports the bundle and stops. It does not control for in-flight spend, since going dark is what changes that spend \cite{angrist2009}.

\section{Software description}
\label{sec:software}

\subsection{Software architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

The functions \texttt{design\_concurrent\_test} and \texttt{disentangle} are built from four modules.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{design.py} assigns markets within groups of similar markets, builds the cell-by-channel flight schedule, and records which channels can be split.
\item \texttt{identified.py} estimates one bundle lift per cell. It also estimates a channel lift when that channel was the only one turned off in a cell.
\item \texttt{exposure.py} checks whether a channel meant to stay on delivered differently in the dark markets than in the business-as-usual markets.
\item \texttt{calibrated.py} fits the impression response and uses each measured bundle lift to guide the channel split.
\end{enumerate}

The bundle estimate removes each market's own baseline and each week's common movement, and it repeats that removal when a market is missing some weeks. Standard errors are clustered by market, so the weeks within one market are treated as one unit and not as separate observations \cite{wooldridge2010,bertrand2004,cameron2015}. The example has 40 markets with 8 dark markets in each cell. On a separate 30-week series of that size, we redrew the assignment 200 times, and the stated 95 percent intervals covered the true cell lift 91.8 percent of the time, slightly below the nominal 95 percent (\texttt{paper/cell\_coverage.py}). For a single cell that is already assigned, \texttt{analyze} can also run time-based regression \cite{kerman2017}, synthetic control \cite{abadie2010,xu2017}, or synthetic difference-in-differences \cite{arkhangelsky2021}. The synthetic-control interval comes from the pre-test residual spread alone and is too narrow for inference, so the package warns when it is used.

\subsection{Software functionalities}
\label{sec:function}

\paragraph{Design}
The user supplies the markets, the groups of similar markets, the channel list, and the channels that go dark in each cell. Within each group, a fraction {\ttfamily control\_\allowbreak share} (default 0.5) of markets stays on business as usual and the rest are split across cells. If a cell map already exists, {\ttfamily design\_\allowbreak from\_\allowbreak assignment} accepts it with the same cell labels. The schedule sets $A_{kc}=1$ when cell $k$ turns channel $c$ off. Each channel is then labeled not tested, tied, separate, or not separable. A channel that the flight never turns off is labeled not tested and gets no response.

\paragraph{Bundle lift}
$D_{it}^{(k)}=1$ when market $i$ is in cell $k$ and week $t$ is in the test window. The cell estimate is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cell}
Y_{it} = \sum_{k} \tau_{k}\, D_{it}^{(k)} + \alpha_{i} + \gamma_{t} + \varepsilon_{it}.
\end{equation}
$\tau_{k}$ is the incremental sales of cell $k$ after removing each market's baseline and each week's common movement. It is a channel estimate only when the cell turned off a single channel. Overlapping bundles can cover every channel, yet the lift still belongs to the whole bundle and cannot be assigned to any one channel in it.

\paragraph{Channels left on}
A channel left on in cell $k$ can still deliver differently in the dark markets than in the business-as-usual markets. The impression gap is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shift}
S_{kc} = \big(\bar X_{c,k,\mathrm{post}} - \bar X_{c,k,\mathrm{pre}}\big) - \big(\bar X_{c,\mathrm{control},\mathrm{post}} - \bar X_{c,\mathrm{control},\mathrm{pre}}\big).
\end{equation}
The pre-test weight $b_{c}$ is the coefficient on channel $c$ in a regression of pre-test sales on all channel impressions with market and week effects, so it reflects the channels' joint response and is not a one-channel estimate. The published cell lift remains $\tau_{k}$. Suppose $|b_c S_{kc}|$ is larger than \texttt{material} times the larger of $|\tau_{k}|$ and the standard deviation of pre-test sales across all markets and weeks. The channel split then uses a refit that accounts for those still-on impressions. The value of \texttt{material} is 0.10 unless the user changes it, and the user can choose to publish the refit as the cell lift. The example table shows the requested refit.

\paragraph{Channel split}
$W_{kc}$ is the impressions of channel $c$ held out of cell $k$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:withheld}
W_{kc} = \max(-S_{kc},\, 0)
\end{equation}
for the channels that the cell turned off. Every other entry in that row is zero. The mix model is asked to make $\sum_{c} \beta_{c} W_{kc}$ close to $-\tau_{k}$, where $\beta_{c}$ is sales per impression. Each $\beta_{c}$ is held at zero when the fit would otherwise slope down. The bundle lift $\tau_{k}$ is not held at zero. This row is a weighted pull toward the lift test and does not act as a hard constraint. The impression history uses every pre-period week, but during the flight it uses only the business-as-usual markets, so dark market-weeks are left out. A more precise bundle lift pulls harder. The bundle table shows whatever gap remains.

The reported uncertainty treats the cell lifts as separate. It does not fully account for shared control markets, or for weeks that enter more than one estimate. The simulations check this shortcut on the design used here and on no other flight.

Two impression paths whose absolute cosine similarity exceeds $1-\epsilon$ are treated as copies, where $\epsilon$ is \texttt{copy\_\allowbreak tolerance} and defaults to $10^{-6}$. One copy stays in the fit so that their shared delivery is still accounted for, and neither copy gets its own response. By default a response is marked unstable if it moves when the calibration pull is weakened, or when the market with the largest in-flight sales is left out. This check is on by default, and the tables below switch it off, so they show point estimates. A blank impression stays blank, the market-day is dropped, and the fit issues a warning. Daily impact is the delivered impressions times $\hat\beta_{c}$. The package can apply geometric adstock. It does not fit a saturation curve, because a single on/off holdout cannot trace a response curve \cite{hanssens2001,jin2017}.

\paragraph{One bundle}
One cell measures a single number, the bundle lift $\tau_{k}$. The held-out impressions $W_{kc}$ tell us how many impressions of each channel were removed, but not how many incremental sales each removed impression was worth. Call that unknown weight $p_{c}$, in sales per impression. The holdout gives one equation,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:one-bundle}
\sum_{c} p_{c} W_{kc} = -\tau_{k}.
\end{equation}
With several channels in the bundle there are more unknowns than equations, and the package does not estimate $p_{c}$.

A user who already has $p_{c}$, from an older single-channel test or from a mix model they are willing to stand behind, can split the measured lift as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:allocation}
a_{kc} = \tau_{k}\,\frac{p_{c} W_{kc}}{\sum_{j} p_{j} W_{kj}}.
\end{equation}
The channel pieces add up to the cell lift. Setting every $p_{c}$ equal to 1 splits the bundle by held-out impressions, and any other weight is an assumption the user brings. We tested this on the public Meridian impression paths with all five channels dark together and sales built from known weights. A split by impressions gives Channel2 7.8 percent of the bundle against a true share of 2.4 percent, and Channel1 12.7 percent against 19.2 percent. Putting the known weights into \eqref{eq:allocation} closes both gaps. This check uses the same weights that built the sales, so the holdout itself does not discover them. The script is \texttt{examples/public\_allocation\_benchmark.py}, and the test suite runs it.

The multi-cell fit described earlier is a different calculation. Because different cells turn off different channels, the impression paths can separate the responses, and when they cannot the response stays blank. Equation \eqref{eq:allocation} is a labeled split of one measured lift and is not a channel incrementality estimate.

The output lists the channel responses, the measured and implied bundle lifts, the still-on check, and daily impact. A market bootstrap reruns the whole pipeline on markets redrawn within each cell.

\section{Illustrative examples}
\label{sec:example}

No public geo holdout comes with a known channel-level incrementality. The example therefore uses Google's published Meridian geo file \path{geo_all_channels.csv}, which has 40 markets and 156 weeks from 25 January 2021 through 15 January 2024 \cite{meridiandata}. The impressions come from that file and the sales are constructed so that the channel responses are known. The example checks the software and says nothing about whether a campaign worked. Meridian's conversions column is used only to group similar markets. It is not used as the outcome because that series already contains Meridian's own media response.

We use three of the five channels, with planted responses $2\times 10^{-4}$, $5\times 10^{-4}$, and $1\times 10^{-4}$ sales per impression. Markets are grouped into quartiles of average conversions. With \texttt{control\_share = 0.4} and seed 1, 16 markets stay on business as usual and 8 go into each cell. The flight is the last 16 weeks, starting 2 October 2023. In a dark market-week the turned-off channel's impressions are set to zero, and sales are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sales}
Y_{it} = \alpha_{i} + \gamma_{t} + \sum_{c} \beta_{c} X_{itc} + \varepsilon_{it},
\end{equation}
with $\alpha_{i}\sim N(0,50^{2})$, $\gamma_{t}\sim N(0,20^{2})$, and $\varepsilon_{it}\sim N(0,30^{2})$, drawn with seed 7. The script is \texttt{paper/example.py}.

The three cells go dark on $\{$Channel0, Channel1$\}$, $\{$Channel0, Channel2$\}$, and $\{$Channel1, Channel2$\}$. No channel is ever dark alone, so a holdout-only table leaves all three channel estimates blank. Table~\ref{tab:slopes} reports the responses from the impression model after it is pulled toward the three measured bundle lifts.

This checks only whether the package recovers the straight-line response it was built to fit. A real flight with a smaller lift will have a wider interval \cite{lewisrao2015}.

\section{Impact}
\label{sec:impact}

As described in Section~\ref{sec:motivation}, GeoLift, GeoX, Robyn and PyMC-Marketing cover parts of this problem \cite{geolift,geox2026,runge2024,pymcmarketing2024}. \texttt{causalgeo} puts three things in one package: a concurrent multi-cell holdout, a report of the gap between the holdout lift and the model, and a blank result for any channel that the impressions cannot separate. California's Proposition 99 panel, a cigarette tax rather than a marketing holdout, checks the single-cell estimator. Synthetic difference-in-differences gives $-15.6$ packs per capita, matching the published figure, and a Nebraska placebo covers zero. It does not check the channel split (\texttt{examples/prop99\_measure.py}).

The current release is 0.2.4. Adoption is early and this paper includes no user study. The script \texttt{paper/reproduce.py} rebuilds all five tables and four figures. A real holdout needs only market outcomes and channel impressions as inputs.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

\texttt{causalgeo} estimates concurrent channel-bundle holdouts and splits a measured bundle only when the impression paths can separate the channels. A single bundled test gives one equation for all the channels in the bundle, so those channel estimates stay blank unless other cells separate them. Splitting that one lift needs sales-per-impression weights that the user supplies. The calibration pulls the mix model toward the lift test and still shows the gap that remains.

On one constructed series the responses are within 0.21 percent of the planted values, when the sales follow that same model, and the calibrated split is tighter than a holdout-only split on that series. The checks also show where the split fails. A response that differs by market cuts coverage to 34.4 percent, a confounder that the holdout leaves on keeps the affected response about 50 percent high, and leaving out carryover or saturation produces large channel errors and large bundle gaps. All of these intervals depend on the response that was fitted and on treating the cells as separate. No real campaign was analyzed and no comparison with other tools was run.

The split is a straight line, with each slope held at zero when it would otherwise be negative, optional geometric adstock, no saturation curve \cite{jin2017}, and no interaction between channels \cite{naik2003}. Market selection and power are left to GeoLift and GeoX, and the package makes no claim that eight markets give a powered cell \cite{geolift,geox2026,lewisrao2015}. In-flight spend is left out of the estimate because the assignment is what changes it \cite{angrist2009,lewis2011}.
