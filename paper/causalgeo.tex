\title{causalgeo: concurrent geo holdouts for bundle lift and calibrated channel responses}

\author[aff1]{Aditya Puttaparthi Tirumala\corref{cor1}}
\ead{puttaparthy.aditya@gmail.com}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\address[aff1]{Independent Researcher}

\begin{abstract}
Marketing teams sometimes turn off different bundles of channels in different groups of markets at the same time. A geo holdout estimates the lift of each bundle. It does not say how to split that lift across the channels inside the bundle. Existing geo tools stop at the bundle. A marketing-mix model can split it, but the split is unstable when the channels move together.

\texttt{causalgeo} is a package for concurrent geo holdouts that reports bundle lift, uses impression paths for a model-assisted channel split, and refuses channel estimates that the design cannot distinguish. One bundled test estimates that bundle's total lift. It does not estimate how many sales each channel's impressions were worth. On one constructed series the recovered responses are within 0.21 percent of the known values.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and significance}
\label{sec:motivation}

\texttt{causalgeo} is a package for concurrent geo holdouts that reports bundle lift, uses impression paths for a model-assisted channel split, and refuses channel estimates that the design cannot distinguish.

A geo test estimates incrementality by comparing markets put on a media change with similar markets left on business as usual \cite{vaver2011,vaver2012}. That comparison avoids the bias in lining up people who saw an ad with people who did not \cite{lewis2011,gordon2019}. The question is simple: did the channel create incremental sales? \cite{blake2015}.

The number of markets that can go dark is usually limited. Take a United States campaign across 210 designated market areas (DMAs). The business may allow a pause in only 30. Large markets may be too risky, some regions may have partner commitments, and others may already be in a flight. Those rules set the holdout pool before anyone looks at balance or power. Advertising lift is small next to ordinary sales movement \cite{lewisrao2015}, so one well-powered single-channel test can use the whole pool. Teams therefore test bundles in several cells at once. Each cell estimates the bundle lift, not the lift of the channels inside it.

GeoLift and Meridian GeoX can design and estimate more than one cell \cite{geolift,geox2026}. They do not split a cell into channels, and they do not tie a marketing-mix model to the measured cell lift. A mix model estimates channel response from impression history, and channels that move together make that split arbitrary \cite{chanperry2017,jin2017}. Robyn and PyMC-Marketing can pull a mix model toward a lift test, including a test of more than one channel \cite{runge2024,pymcmarketing2024}. Pulling toward a test can steady a split \cite{zhang2023}. It cannot create information the test and the impression data do not contain. The split below also assumes the bundle lift is the sum of the channel lifts. If the channels interact, these equations do not name the separate responses \cite{naik2003}.

The package estimates the bundle lift from the holdout, then uses that lift to guide a straight-line model of channel impressions. The channel responses use both the test and the model. They are not channel-level holdout estimates. If the impressions cannot separate the channels, the package reports the bundle and stops. It does not fill the gap with a sales-per-impression weight of its own. It does not control for in-flight spend, because going dark is what changes that spend \cite{angrist2009}.

\section{Software description}
\label{sec:software}

\subsection{Software architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

The workflow behind \texttt{design\_concurrent\_test} and \texttt{disentangle} has four parts.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{design.py} assigns markets inside groups of similar markets, builds the cell-by-channel flight schedule, and says which channels can be split.
\item \texttt{identified.py} estimates one bundle lift per cell, and a channel lift only when that channel was the only one turned off.
\item \texttt{exposure.py} checks whether a channel that was supposed to stay on delivered differently in the dark markets and the business-as-usual markets.
\item \texttt{calibrated.py} fits the impression response and uses each measured bundle lift to guide the channel split.
\end{enumerate}

The bundle estimate takes out each market's own baseline and each week's common movement, repeating that removal when a market misses some weeks. The uncertainty is by market: weeks inside one market count as one market, not as separate observations \cite{wooldridge2010,bertrand2004,cameron2015}. This example has 40 markets and 8 dark markets in each cell. On a separate series of that size, over 30 weeks, redrawing the assignment 200 times, the stated 95 percent intervals covered the true cell lift 91.8 percent of the time. For a single cell that is already assigned, \texttt{analyze} can also run time-based regression \cite{kerman2017}, synthetic control \cite{abadie2010,xu2017}, or synthetic difference-in-differences \cite{arkhangelsky2021}. GeoLift and GeoX still own market selection and the power search \cite{geolift,geox2026}.

\subsection{Software functionalities}
\label{sec:function}

\paragraph{Design}
The user supplies the markets, groups of similar markets, the channel list, and which channels go dark in each cell. Inside each group, {\ttfamily control\_\allowbreak share} stays on business as usual. The other markets are split across cells. {\ttfamily design\_\allowbreak from\_\allowbreak assignment} takes a cell map that already exists, with the same cell labels. The schedule marks $A_{kc}=1$ when cell $k$ turns channel $c$ off. Each channel is then called not tested, tied, separate, or not separable. A channel the flight never turns off stays not tested and gets no response.

\paragraph{Bundle lift}
$D_{it}^{(k)}=1$ when market $i$ is in cell $k$ and week $t$ is in the test window. The cell estimate is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cell}
Y_{it} = \sum_{k} \tau_{k}\, D_{it}^{(k)} + \alpha_{i} + \gamma_{t} + \varepsilon_{it}.
\end{equation}
$\tau_{k}$ is that cell's incremental sales, after each market's baseline and each week's common movement. It is a channel estimate only when the cell turned one channel off. Overlapping bundles can cover every channel and the bundle lift still belongs to the bundle, not to one channel inside it.

\paragraph{Channels left on}
A channel left on in cell $k$ can still deliver differently in the dark markets than in the business-as-usual markets. That impression gap is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shift}
S_{kc} = \big(\bar X_{c,k,\mathrm{post}} - \bar X_{c,k,\mathrm{pre}}\big) - \big(\bar X_{c,\mathrm{control},\mathrm{post}} - \bar X_{c,\mathrm{control},\mathrm{pre}}\big).
\end{equation}
The pre-test weight $b_{c}$ is the joint response of the channels, not an estimate of sales on one channel. The published cell lift stays $\tau_{k}$. If $|b_c S_{kc}|$ is more than \texttt{material} times the larger of $|\tau_{k}|$ and the usual week-to-week sales movement before the test, the channel split uses a refit that accounts for those still-on impressions. \texttt{material} is 0.10 unless the user changes it. The user can also publish that refit. Channels that were turned off are the test. They are not controls. The example table shows the requested refit.

\paragraph{Channel split}
$W_{kc}$ is the impressions of channel $c$ held out of cell $k$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:withheld}
W_{kc} = \max(-S_{kc},\, 0)
\end{equation}
for channels that cell turned off. Every other entry in that row is zero. The mix model should make $\sum_{c} \beta_{c} W_{kc}$ close to $-\tau_{k}$, where $\beta_{c}$ is sales per impression. That row is a weighted pull toward the lift test. It is not a hard constraint. The impression history uses every pre-period week, and only the business-as-usual markets during the flight. Dark market-weeks stay out of that history. A more precise bundle lift pulls harder. The bundle table shows whatever gap is left.

The reported uncertainty treats the cell lifts as separate. It does not fully account for shared control markets or weeks that enter more than one estimate. The simulations check that shortcut on this design, not on every flight.

Impression paths closer than $1-\texttt{copy\_tolerance}$ are treated as copies. The default tolerance is $10^{-6}$. One copy stays in the fit so their shared delivery is not ignored, and neither copy gets its own response. By default a response is marked unstable if it moves when the calibration pull is weakened, or when the market with the largest in-flight sales is left out. The tables below turn that check off, so the numbers are the point estimates. A blank impression stays blank, that market-day is dropped, and the fit warns. Daily impact is delivered impressions times $\hat\beta_{c}$. The package can apply geometric adstock. It does not fit a saturation curve, because one on/off holdout cannot trace the response curve \cite{hanssens2001,jin2017}.

\paragraph{One bundle}
One cell measures one number: the bundle lift $\tau_{k}$. The held-out impressions $W_{kc}$ say how many impressions of each channel were removed. They do not say how many incremental sales each removed impression was worth. Call that unknown weight $p_{c}$, in sales per impression. The holdout gives one equation,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:one-bundle}
\sum_{c} p_{c} W_{kc} = -\tau_{k}.
\end{equation}
Several channels in the same bundle leave this one equation short. The package does not estimate $p_{c}$.

If the user already has $p_{c}$, from an older single-channel test or from a mix model they are willing to own, the measured lift can be split as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:allocation}
a_{kc} = \tau_{k}\,\frac{p_{c} W_{kc}}{\sum_{j} p_{j} W_{kj}}.
\end{equation}
The channel pieces add back to the cell lift. Setting every $p_{c}$ equal to 1 splits the bundle by held-out impressions. Any other weight is an assumption the user brings in. On the public Meridian impression paths, with all five channels dark together and sales built from known weights, an impression split gives Channel2 7.8 percent of the bundle against a true share of 2.4 percent, and Channel1 12.7 percent against 19.2 percent. Putting those known weights into \eqref{eq:allocation} closes the gaps. The check uses the same weights that built the sales. The holdout does not discover them. The script is \texttt{examples/public\_allocation\_benchmark.py}.

The multi-cell fit above is a different calculation. Different cells turn off different channels, so the impression paths can separate the responses. When they cannot, the response stays blank. Equation \eqref{eq:allocation} is a labeled split of one measured lift. It is not a channel incrementality estimate.

The output is the channel responses, the measured lift next to the lift implied by those responses, the still-on impression check, and daily impact. A market bootstrap reruns the whole pipeline on markets redrawn inside each cell.

\section{Illustrative examples}
\label{sec:example}

No public geo holdout comes with a known channel-level incrementality. The example uses Google's published Meridian geo file \path{geo_all_channels.csv}: 40 markets and 156 weeks, from 25 January 2021 through 15 January 2024 \cite{meridiandata}. The public file demonstrates that the software runs. The sales are constructed so the channel responses are known. That is a check of the software, not evidence that a campaign worked. Meridian's conversions column is used only to group similar markets. It is not the outcome, because that series already contains Meridian's own media response.

Three of the five channels are used, with planted responses $2\times 10^{-4}$, $5\times 10^{-4}$, and $1\times 10^{-4}$ sales per impression. Markets are grouped into quartiles of average conversions. With \texttt{control\_share = 0.4} and seed 1, 16 markets stay on business as usual and 8 go into each cell. The flight is the last 16 weeks, starting 2 October 2023. In a dark market-week the turned-off channel's impressions are set to zero, and sales are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sales}
Y_{it} = \alpha_{i} + \gamma_{t} + \sum_{c} \beta_{c} X_{itc} + \varepsilon_{it},
\end{equation}
with $\alpha_{i}\sim N(0,50^{2})$, $\gamma_{t}\sim N(0,20^{2})$, and $\varepsilon_{it}\sim N(0,30^{2})$, drawn with seed 7. The script is \texttt{paper/example.py}.

The cells go dark on $\{$Channel0, Channel1$\}$, $\{$Channel0, Channel2$\}$, and $\{$Channel1, Channel2$\}$. No channel is dark alone, so the holdout-only table leaves all three channel estimates blank. Table~\ref{tab:slopes} reports the impression-model responses, pulled toward the three measured bundle lifts.

This checks whether the package recovers the straight-line response it was built to fit. It is not evidence about saturation, long carryover, or whether eight markets are enough to power a cell. A real flight with a smaller lift will have a wider interval \cite{lewisrao2015}.

\section{Impact}
\label{sec:impact}

\texttt{causalgeo} is a package for concurrent geo holdouts that reports bundle lift, uses impression paths for a model-assisted channel split, and refuses channel estimates that the design cannot distinguish. GeoLift and GeoX can run more than one cell. They do not make this split \cite{geolift,geox2026}. Robyn and PyMC-Marketing already calibrate a mix model to lift tests \cite{runge2024,pymcmarketing2024}. This package is the concurrent holdout, the reported gap, and the blank channel when the impressions cannot separate it. The bundle lift is the holdout. A channel response is reported when the cells separate the channels. Splitting one bundle with a sales-per-impression weight is an assumption the user brings, and the package does not estimate that weight.

The current release is 0.2.2. Adoption is early, and this paper has no user study. \texttt{paper/reproduce.py} rebuilds all five tables and four figures. The same functions apply to a real holdout once the user supplies market outcomes and channel impressions. Channels the flight cannot separate, or whose impressions are copies, stay blank rather than receiving a made-up response.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

\texttt{causalgeo} estimates concurrent channel-bundle holdouts and splits a measured bundle only when the impression paths can separate the channels. One bundled test estimates that bundle's lift. The impressions removed from each channel are observed. The sales per impression are not. Equation \eqref{eq:one-bundle} is one equation for every channel in the bundle, so those channel estimates stay blank unless other cells separate them. Equation \eqref{eq:allocation} splits the measured lift only after the user supplies the sales-per-impression weights. Those weights are an input. They are not an estimate from the holdout. The calibration is a pull, not a force: it moves the mix model toward the lift test and still shows the gap. On one constructed series the responses are within 0.21 percent of the planted values. Solving the same cell lifts with no impression history gives uncertainty of 0.43 to 1.14 sales per 10{,}000 impressions, against 0.006 to 0.008 for the calibrated split. Across 200 draws, four times the leftover sales noise raises median error from 0.19 to 0.76 percent and leaves coverage at 95.8 percent. A response that differs by market cuts coverage to 34.4 percent. A confounder the holdout leaves on keeps the affected response about 50 percent high. Nearly copied channels with small effects move from 44.6 percent error to 10.5 percent once the cell lifts are included. A carryover fit that uses the decay from the sales gives 94.2 percent coverage. Leaving out carryover or saturation produces large channel errors and large bundle gaps. On a 30-week series with the same market counts, the cell intervals covered the true lift in 91.8 percent of 200 redrawn assignments. The intervals still depend on the response that was fitted, and on treating the cells as separate.

The split is a straight line, with optional geometric adstock and no saturation curve \cite{jin2017}. Turning a channel on or off does not trace the response curve, so the package does not fit one, and it does not estimate an interaction between channels \cite{naik2003}. GeoLift and GeoX handle market selection and power \cite{geolift,geox2026}. This package assigns markets inside groups the user has already chosen, or accepts that assignment, and does not claim that eight markets make a powered cell \cite{lewisrao2015}. The published fits leave the stability check off. The default cell number is the unadjusted holdout. When lost impressions move onto a channel left on, the split uses the refitted cell lift after that delivery is removed. In-flight spend stays out of the estimate because the assignment is what changes it \cite{angrist2009,lewis2011}.
